Create a calm, tactile poster from the supplied photo. Design intent (internal, never drawn as text): 真景为锚、插画成场、色彩成结构、撕纸成界、纸面会呼吸 — keep the photographic scene truthful; let a larger abstract illustration field reinterpret selected source elements instead of tracing them; compress foliage, branches, leaves, crowds, and micro-detail into a few large quiet forms; make the added hue share source-derived shapes with the illustration instead of floating as decoration; keep a visible hand-torn fibrous edge where photography becomes paper; make omission and negative space active parts of the illustration.

Priority: preserve scene identity and key spatial relationships → keep the photographic portion truthful → simplify complex organic/repetitive detail into a few large legible masses and directional gestures → the illustration is a large designed field, not a literal copy or peripheral echo → photography, illustration, and the added hue share one source-derived compositional skeleton → the hue changes balance, movement, figure–ground, or meaning → substantial quiet space → tactile hand-torn photo edge at the primary material handoff → one restrained micro-text element. Preserve relationships before details; remove detail before adding decoration.

## Scene card

Resolve first: 1–2 core subjects that make the scene identifiable; 2–3 supporting elements; spatial invariants that must survive (horizon, relative positions, scale, perspective, facing direction, path, silhouette, overlap); dominant gesture (strongest horizontal, vertical, diagonal, curve, convergence, gaze, movement); visual-weight map (area, darkness, saturation, texture, faces, isolation, edge tension); native color atmosphere (hue family, temperature, value range, existing saturated areas); 1–2 source-shape candidates (silhouettes, planes, shadows, paths, architectural rhythms) that can become both illustration and color structure; natural quiet areas (sky, water, wall, ground, haze); the semantic minimum — the smallest combination of forms that still identifies this particular scene.

## Abstraction

Medium abstraction by default: keep the core subject, dominant gesture, one key spatial relationship; remove ~60–80% of small descriptive detail; merge repeated or adjacent forms into larger masses; replace realistic shading with flat ink, broken contour, sparse hatching, halftone, or paper cutout; allow departure from literal scale/crop while the scene stays identifiable; keep at least one unmistakable source-specific feature. For every illustrated section decide: Retain (≤1–2 defining forms) / Merge (repeated trees, railings, windows, waves, crowds into one rhythm or silhouette) / Omit (secondary objects, surface detail, clutter) / Transform (flat silhouette, broken contour, geometric cutout, ink field, repeated mark) / Expose (deliberately blank paper). No full-scene tracing with vintage texture on top.

One primary illustration grammar + ≤1 supporting: silhouette-led (one broad dark/gray mass — figures, trees, roofs, boats) / contour-led (few broken lines — architecture, paths, coastlines) / field-led (irregular ink or halftone field — water, fog, sky, shadow) / rhythm-led (repeated marks — posts, windows, branches, crowds) / cut-paper-led (1–2 simplified cutouts for strong shape hierarchies).

Foliage compression: dense trees, needles, leaves, vines, grass, flowers, hair-like branches, crowds, gravel must become calmer in illustration than in photography. In a foliage-dominant area keep one main canopy or tree mass, 1–3 directional branch gestures, ≤2 secondary clusters, one sparse texture sample; omit ~85–95% of individual leaves, needles, fine twigs, repeated organic marks. Silhouette- or cut-paper-led by default for trees; rhythm-led only as sparse supporting trace, never a carpet of detail. Represent foliage as grouped masses, broken lobes, clipped shadow shapes, short interrupted rhythms — never leaf by leaf. Preserve the source-specific lean, canopy opening, branch direction, or light gap, not botanical detail. Merge overlapping trees into one dominant mass + ≤1 subordinate counter-mass. If it reads as lace, filigree, or a botanical print at thumbnail size, merge and remove marks. Complexity in the photo is a reason to simplify more, not to print more.

Density: separate field extent from printed density. Illustration influences ~45–70% of the poster with much of that field visibly unprinted; keep ~55–75% of the illustration field quiet (65–85% for foliage-dominant or intricate scenes, active illustration then ~10–25% of the poster; otherwise active ink/cut-paper/halftone ~15–35%). One dominant illustration mass large enough to affect the overall silhouette, +1–2 supporting marks, one restrained texture field. ≤2 neutral ink values besides paper tone and the single added hue. Prefer incomplete edges, interrupted contours, cropped forms, internal negative space. Blank paper separates ideas; keep any speckles or ghost marks confined to the photo–illustration transition. Enlarge before adding detail; the result reads at thumbnail and feels intentionally unfinished at normal size.

Scene fidelity: preserve relative position and direction of core subjects, the dominant horizon/path/shoreline/gaze/silhouette, 1–2 source-specific shapes, and the original tension between heavy and quiet areas. The illustration may simplify anatomy, texture, scale, minor perspective; it may not invent unrelated scenery or replace the spatial logic with generic motifs.

## Composition

Vertical 3:5 paper-poster canvas unless the user requests otherwise. Figure–ground legibility, balance of visual weight (not equal area), one focal area, movement guided by source lines and color sequence, dominant–subordinate hierarchy (no accidental 50/50), directional breathing room in front of gazes and diagonals. Layout families (correct by visual weight, never sacrifice identity for a number): transformative seam — photo ~30–50%, one real subject/horizon/path/shadow continues across the boundary into a larger illustration field; underprint overlay — photo ~35–60%, broad low-density illustration and chromatic underprint extend behind and partly through the photo without damaging defining detail; photo anchor + expansive field — photo ~25–45%, field ~45–70%; directional split — ~55/45 to 65/35 aligned to a real horizon, gaze, path, or structural edge with selective crossing; irregular fragments — combined photo fragments ~25–50%, core subject in the largest, connected by one shared illustration mass or chromatic structure. Avoid defaulting to centered photo with text beneath.

## Torn edge

The photo–paper boundary is a real paper object: a visible hand-torn fibrous edge is the default and primary transition at the main handoff. Build it with an irregular hand-ripped contour (shallow notches, uneven rises, soft scallops, occasional longer fiber pulls), a narrow feathered fringe of exposed fibers and warm paper tone, slight local thinning/abrasion/dry pigment loss, natural asymmetry (some segments nearly straight, some ragged, 1–2 stronger pressure points), flat scan behavior with no artificial lifted-paper depth. The active fibrous band is ~1–4% of the poster's short edge; the torn contour is legible along ~35–70% of the visible photographic perimeter — not a uniform frame, not almost invisible. Illustration or chromatic structure may cross, emerge from, or pass behind selected torn segments while the tear itself stays readable. Speckled dissolve, halftone crumbs, or 1–3 faint ghost marks only as subordinate residue at 1–2 pressure points; irregular scale and spacing; symbols stay incomplete, faint, nonverbal, print-derived; colors: paper tone, graphite, charcoal, faded brown-black, or the existing single chromatic hue — no second hue. No clean digital clipping, crisp rectangular masks, sticker borders, decorative deckled frames, heavy drop shadows, curled corners, thick layered-paper depth, or torn effects on every edge.

## Chromatic structure

One added print hue as compositional structure — natural colors in the photographic section don't count. Choose by relationship: quiet harmony (saturated analogous), focused counterpoint (complementary), temperature bridge (warm hue in a cool scene or the reverse), source resonance (intensify a meaningful minor source color). Name an exact color (saturated cobalt blue, opaque ultramarine, clean tomato red, vivid pear green, lemon yellow, saturated magenta-pink); judge it beside source colors, paper tone, and neutral inks; never weaken it with pale/muted/faded/pastel/low-saturation wording. Integration modes — pick one, plus one function (focal reinforcement, counterweight, photo–illustration bridge, eye-path direction, figure–ground clarification, semantic emphasis): source continuation (a roofline, road, cloud, window, garment, shadow, or vehicle panel continues beyond the photographic edge); selective replacement (one real photographic region becomes a flat high-chroma shape); underprint passage (broad translucent/halftone/misregistered field behind and partly through both); counterform (colored positive/negative shape from a source silhouette, gap, shadow, or quiet area); directional rhythm (repeat or stretch one source-derived colored form along a gaze, path, railing, ridge, shoreline). The structure must pass ≥2 tests: derive its contour/position/rhythm from the scene; touch, overlap, replace, pass behind or through photography or illustration; cross or transform at the photo–paper boundary; redirect the eye path or rebalance weight; reveal a real subject, relationship, atmosphere, or tension. Never a detached rectangle, corner patch, generic circle, arbitrary bright dot, or isolated swatch. Optional 1–2 smaller source-anchored echoes, subordinate. Area by material: opaque replacement or cut-paper form ~2–6% of the poster; translucent/halftone/misregistered underprint ~6–15%; large structural color field ~10–20% (lower opacity and ink density at this range). Lower end when the photo is already vivid; upper end when the hue carries structure. Preserve high chroma through grain, ink bleed, broken coverage, slight misregistration — never neon or glossy digital color. Structural removal test: mentally remove the hue; if eye path, balance, figure–ground, continuity, and interpretation stay essentially unchanged, redesign it.

## Micro-text

One subtle text element that feels discovered on the paper, never a headline. Supplied wording is reproduced exactly — no translation, expansion, rewriting, subtitle, or attribution. Otherwise author one compact line from the scene: an observed feeling, not metadata. Language: English-only by default; Chinese-only or Chinese–English pairing only when the user supplies or requests it. Limits: Chinese ≤8 Han characters; English ≤5 words; bilingual applies both limits independently with semantically connected lines. English forms: one standalone word (Horizon, Rain, Stillness), a keyword sequence of 2–4 words with one repeated separator (comma, `·`, `/`, or `&` — never mixed; e.g. `Cloud / Ridge / Silence`), or a very short phrase (After the rain). Concrete scene language for documentary photos; emotional fragment for solitary figures, dusk, rain, travel, waiting. No dates, addresses, coordinates, archive stamps, or invented quotations with attribution. Size ~1.5–3.5% of poster height for Chinese, ~1.3–2.8% for English — clearly subordinate. Lettering: lightly imperfect handwriting, typewriter-like letterpress, faint pencil, worn stamp, or dry ink — one treatment; English prefers a small vintage typewriter serif or monospaced mechanical face, sentence case or quiet title case, never polished digital display or bold all-caps; bilingual: Chinese faint handwriting/pencil/Song-style, English the smaller typewriter line, one shared alignment and ink family. Integrate into the paper: uneven pressure, broken ink, soft edge wear, same flat scanned texture. Ink value: charcoal, warm gray, faded brown-black, muted graphite, or a restrained echo of the added hue — never a bright new color; characters remain legible and correctly spelled. Placement: an existing quiet-paper area beneath, beside, or offset from the illustration — not on a face, defining subject, or dense printed area; aligned to a source edge, torn edge, axis, or baseline with generous breathing room; single line by default (two related lines for bilingual with clear primary–secondary hierarchy); the text is the eye path's final resting point, below the chromatic structure in weight. No decorative rules, icons, labels, serial numbers, or timestamps.
