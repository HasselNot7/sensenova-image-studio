Create a calm, flat-scanned paper poster whose three layers stay clearly different: 1) photographic anchor — the supplied image, unchanged inside the retained area; 2) registered illustration — a sparse print interpretation of the exact scene hidden beneath paper; 3) guide color — one high-saturation source-derived line or compact field that directs the eye. Design intent (internal, never drawn as text): 真景不改、同位简译、源色渐隐、艳色引路、颗粒消融. Priorities: scene identity and input orientation → photo anchor unchanged → spatial continuity between photo and illustration → simplify before adding texture → source color used twice (rich-to-quiet fade inside the illustration; one high-saturation family for eye guidance) → fuse through pigment dissolution, never a torn or cut edge → text short, readable, subordinate. When priorities conflict, protect the photograph and remove illustration detail.

## Scene card

Resolve first: 1–2 core subjects identifying the scene; spatial invariants (horizon, scale, relative position, overlap, facing direction, perspective, contact points); dominant gesture (strongest horizon, diagonal, gaze, path, shoreline, roofline, wire, branch, movement); visual-weight map (large dark areas, faces, bright windows, saturated regions, isolation, edge tension); native palette (dominant temperature + 2–4 broad local hue families); color-fade direction (the path from photographic boundary through illustrated scene to quiet paper); guide-color candidate (one meaningful source hue + one source path it can follow); quiet field (sky, water, wall, ground, haze); semantic minimum.

## Photographic anchor — zero restyling

Retained photographic regions are an immutable plate: cropping, placement, and a boundary dissolve are allowed; interior alteration is not. Preserve the source's existing color, white balance, saturation, exposure, contrast, shadows, highlights; softness, focus, resolution, grain, noise, haze, lens character, motion blur; geometry, silhouettes, texture, object detail; identity, expression, gaze, posture, proportions, skin tone, facial softness. Do not apply color grading, desaturation, recoloring, relighting, HDR, dehaze, local contrast, clarity, sharpening, texture enhancement, denoise-reconstruct, synthetic upscaling, AI smoothing, beautification, skin retouching, face enhancement. Do not invent absent detail — eyes, teeth, hair, pores, fabric weave, ripples, foliage, reflections, signage, architecture. No paper texture, halftone, illustration marks, or vintage filters over the photo interior. Faces stay protected even when small; a soft face remains soft.

## Spatially registered illustration

Every paper-covered location depicts the real source content hidden beneath it: same content (sky stays sky; a building, person, flag, tree, fence, pole, road stays that object); same coordinates (approximate position, scale, crop, overlap order); same geometry (horizon height, slopes, wire and rail angles, perspective convergence, rhythm, facing direction, contact base); same continuation (a contour crossing from photography into paper starts at the same point and continues in the same direction); lower information density — omit ~65–85% of small detail; spatial registration is not tracing. One primary grammar + ≤1 support: contour-led (broken lines — architecture, paths, rails, coastlines, wires, gestures) / field-led (sparse halftone or translucent ink — sky, water, fog, shadow, ground) / silhouette-led (1–2 broad masses — people, trees, roofs, boats) / rhythm-led (few repeated marks — windows, posts, waves, steps). Keep the illustration visibly quieter than photography: merge repeated forms, leave contours incomplete, preserve generous blank paper. No pores, facial features beyond the semantic minimum, hair strands, fabric weave, individual leaves, gravel, micro-ripples, or photographic shading. Dense foliage: omit 85–95% of individual parts; keep one dominant mass, 1–3 directional gestures, ≤2 secondary clusters.

## Source-color gradient

The illustration keeps the covered scene's color and loses it gradually across space — never pale the moment photography ends. Keep 2–4 broad source hue families in their correct locations (hue direction and temperature matter more than exact samples). One continuous fade path from the photographic boundary to quiet paper, following scene depth, atmosphere, or the dominant gesture — not a mechanical straight band. Near zone: retain ~70–85% of the source's perceived saturation and value contrast — illustrated, but unmistakably connected to the photo. Middle zone: ~45–60%, merged into broad washes, broken risograph layers, dry ink, pencil, halftone. Far zone: ~20–35%, thinner printed density, dissolving into paper tone. Vary saturation, value contrast, and mark density together so it reads as layered atmospheric loss, not three visible stripes. The illustration stays simpler and flatter than photography at every stage; high local saturation never restores photographic texture. Never make the whole illustration uniformly faint, beige, gray, or washed out; never move color to an unrelated object for decoration. Correct registration: cyan water stays with water, cobalt ice shadow with ice, vegetation green with vegetation, warm window light with windows, garment color with the same figure.

## High-saturation eye guide

One clearly visible high-saturation family derived from a meaningful source color — guiding attention, not decorating empty space. Choose the hue by: intensifying a meaningful minor source color, an analogous saturated extension of the native atmosphere, a near-complementary connection between separated focal areas, or bridging warm and cool areas. One exact family (coral-magenta from sunset pink, cobalt from deep water, tomato red from a garment, vivid green from vegetation); no second competing bright family. Path: derive the geometry from the source's dominant gesture — follow and transform along a horizon, shoreline, roofline, path, gaze, rail, wire, branch, or shadow; begin near one focal point, disappear through a quiet interval, reappear near the next; one broken line plus 2–3 compact flat passages in the same family; pass behind or beside subjects without recoloring, covering, outlining, or altering them. Area ~4–10% of the poster; matte risograph, dry ink, broken coverage, or slight misregistration. At thumbnail size, removing it must weaken the eye path, balance, or continuity. Never a detached corner block, generic circle, arbitrary bright dot, isolated swatch, confetti, uniform border, or sticker.

## Composition and fusion

Portrait source → portrait 3:5; landscape → landscape 5:3; square → portrait 3:5 unless the dominant gesture needs width. Layouts: transformative seam (photo 30–50%, a real contour or plane continues into illustration); photo anchor + expansive field (photo 25–45%, registered illustration and quiet paper carry the wider composition); underprint overlay (photo 35–60%, photo interior untouched while illustration and guide color move around or behind it); directional split (start near 55/45 to 65/35, aligned to a real source gesture). Balance visual weight, not equal area; breathing room in front of gazes and diagonals; enlarge the illustrated field by extent, never by added detail.

Fusion: one asymmetric pigment-dissolution zone around the photographic anchor. Begin the transition before the photo would otherwise stop; only inside this boundary zone taper contrast, saturation, micro-detail, and edge certainty; break the remaining image into soft halftone dust, dry-brush residue, translucent pigment loss, paper-fiber absorption; let the registered illustration emerge from the same geometry; keep horizons, paths, rails, wires, shorelines, figures, and crossing points aligned; residue thins asymmetrically into clean paper, never a closed outline. Broader dissolve for sky, fog, water, ground; narrower around faces, hands, thin limbs, recognizable objects, decisive structural edges. Never increase or reconstruct detail before dissolving it. Hard prohibition: torn edge, deckled rim, exposed fiber strip, ripped notch, scallop, paper frame, cutout outline, white halo, glow, uniform blur rim, sticker edge, rectangular clipping, drop shadow, curled corner, lifted paper, 3D depth.

## Text material

One short readable unit with a spatial behavior, not a caption. Reproduce supplied wording exactly; otherwise author one English-only scene phrase: one word, a 2–4-word keyword sequence, or ≤5 words; Chinese-only or bilingual only when supplied or requested, Chinese ≤8 Han characters. Choose one primary behavior: semantic break (phrase split at a pause — image contains a seam or threshold); drifting baseline (fragments rise, fall, or arc along source movement — wind, water, walking); offset/misregistration (one small echo shifted — photocopy, risograph, memory, reflection); two-scale contrast (one word larger, one small response); directional alignment (type along a horizon, rail, gaze, road, shadow); selective occlusion (one image edge crosses part of a fragment — reading order stays obvious). Combine ≤2 behaviors only from the same source gesture. Reading order obvious through proximity, value, scale, or direction; all words fully spelled, characters complete; never scatter letters; if partially covered, keep word beginnings and ends visible. ≤2 type voices and 2 size levels; pair voices with a reason (typewriter + faint pencil, dry ink + monospaced print, restrained Song-style + small mechanical English); ink values in one paper-derived family (charcoal, graphite, faded brown-black, or a restrained echo of the guide hue). Text attaches to an image edge, enters a quiet field, passes behind a subject, or emerges from illustration — never merely sits beneath; it may not conceal a face or destroy the semantic minimum; blank paper is part of the textual rhythm; at thumbnail it reads as a deliberate material event, at normal size every word is readable. No polished display typography, ≥3 fonts, bold advertising hierarchy, decorative title cases, random size changes.

## Hard avoids

No torn edge, deckled rim, fiber strip, ripped notch, scallop, paper frame, cutout outline, white halo, glow, blur rim, sticker edge, rectangular clipping, drop shadow, curled corner, lifted paper, or 3D. No interior photo restyling of any kind. No literal traced illustration, photorealistic illustration, invented hidden content, wrong coordinates or perspective, uniform faint illustration, saturation stripes, decorative guide color, detached color blocks, arbitrary dots, multiple bright families, scattered letters, illegible text, polished digital typography, logos, CTA, neon, 3D depth, cinematic lighting, watermarks.
